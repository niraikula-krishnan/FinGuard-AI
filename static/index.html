FinGuard Glossary

A quick reference guide to all financial and risk terms used in this application.

Tenure

The number of years over which a loan is repaid. A longer tenure means smaller monthly payments but more total interest paid. Example: 5-year tenure = 60 EMI payments.

Debt-to-Income (DTI)

The ratio of total monthly debt payments to gross monthly income. A DTI above 40% is considered risky. Lower is better.

DTI = (Monthly Debt / Monthly Income) x 100

Credit Score

A 3-digit number (300-850) representing creditworthiness. Based on payment history, debt levels, and credit age. Higher is better — 750+ is considered excellent.

300-579 Poor 580-669 Fair 670-739 Good 800+ Excellent

EMI (Payment per Month)

Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed monthly amount to repay a loan. Calculated using loan amount, 10.5% annual interest rate, and tenure.

EMI = P x r x (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1)

Risk Score

FinGuard's default probability score (0-100%). Calculated from credit score, DTI, employment status, and loan-to-income ratio. Higher = more likely to default.

0-25% Low Risk 25-50% Moderate 50%+ High Risk

AML Compliance

Anti-Money Laundering checks scan the applicant notes and financial profile for red flags such as anonymous accounts, crypto wash trading, and suspicious cash flows. Result: PASSED or FLAGGED.

Employment Status

The borrower's current work situation. Fully Employed carries the lowest risk. Unemployed significantly increases the default risk score as there is no guaranteed income stream.

Audit Report

A detailed AI-generated or rule-based analysis of the loan application. It summarizes risk factors, compliance checks, and provides a recommendation on whether the application should be approved or rejected.
